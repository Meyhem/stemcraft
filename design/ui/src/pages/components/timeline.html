Timeline & loop

Bars are the unit the player thinks in, so bars are the unit the UI shows. Underneath, the beat grid in analysis.json is integer sample indices at 48 kHz (D-03) — never float seconds. Loops are stored in song.json as bar numbers, which survive a re-analysis, and are resolved to sample offsets through the grid at load.

The playhead is positioned by requestAnimationFrame from the engine's clock. Each lane is painted from the stem's precomputed envelope onto a canvas the size of the visible part of the lane, which repaints its slice on every pan — so the cost is the same at every zoom. Nothing that draws a waveform plays audio (D-07), and none has a playhead of its own to disagree with.

The ruler is the one click-to-seek strip: a tinted band with a pointer cursor that brightens on hover. Everywhere below it a press means something else (zoom here, a cut in the Album splitter), so the two must never look alike.

Zoom is continuous, from the whole song fitted to the view down to 200 px per second (stem peaks hold 100 buckets/s, so that is 2 px a bucket — fine enough to put a loop on a beat). The readout says how much is in view, in bars when the song is analysed and as time when it is not; px/s means nothing to a player.

Follow playhead pages the view while playing. A zoom or pan by hand while playing switches it off, visibly (the toggle unlights), so the view never snaps back while you look elsewhere. While paused, follow moves the view only when the playhead itself moves — a seek, or switching follow on — never because the zoom changed.

Why looping never seeks

D-06: the engine wraps its own read cursor in the input domain with a ~5 ms crossfade, so the shared stretcher never learns a loop happened. Seeking the stretcher would emit buffered loop-end audio after every jump — a click on each repetition, which N-05 forbids. Nothing in this UI may expose a control that seeks during an active loop; scrubbing the waveform while looping disarms the loop first, and the loop button visibly unlights.
