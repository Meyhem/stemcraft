Tab staff

The Tab screen's main content: the bass line the worker transcribed, as tablature on the same time axis as the Stems view, with its ruler, chord row, loop region, playhead and one zoom-and-pan model (U-11, U-12). Four lines, highest string on top, as on every neck (U-13). A note is a fret chip whose left edge is its onset, with a tail to where it stops ringing. The chip is never snapped to the beat grid: transcription is not quantised, so the drawing is not either, and a note that sits a little late looks a little late. The staff is painted on a viewport-sized canvas in the app, slaved to the engine clock like every other painter (D-07).

Note kinds (U-16)

Transcribed · bass hue (the stem it came from, U-01), fret in mono on the chip.
Sounding now · accent, with a halo. "Act on this" is accent on every neck (U-13).
Unsure · the tracker's confidence was below the floor. Same hue at 40 %, and a ? on the fret, so it reads without colour.
Substituted · warn ring and what changed: ↑8 raised an octave because, after the pitch shift, it fell below the open E.

Rhythm as played · 4 bars at 280 px per bar

Bar lines bright, beat lines faint, as on the Stems lanes. In the third bar the repeated G lands a little after the "and" of 2, so it is drawn there. Overlapping notes stay on their own strings; two notes on one string at once cannot happen, because the transcription is monophonic.

Zoomed out · 8 bars at 120 px per bar

The chip keeps its size and only the gaps shrink, so zoomed out a fast run touches and overlaps, the later note drawn on top. That is enough to find your place; zoom in to read the frets. Zoom stays continuous from Fit to the axis maximum, as on every time axis (U-12): no separate floor for the staff.

What the staff never does

It never hides an unsure note, never snaps one to the grid, and never moves a note to another string to "fix" it. Every change from what was heard is drawn as a substitution with its reason (N-08). It never plays audio (D-07), and it is not editable in this version.
